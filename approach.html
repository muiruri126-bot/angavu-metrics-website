Our Approach

Evidence-driven, participatory, and adaptive — how we design, deliver, and learn.

Pillar 1

Evidence-Driven and Context-Sensitive

Good evidence is necessary but not sufficient — it must be relevant, timely, and presented in ways that decision-makers can use. We begin every engagement by understanding the client's decision-making context: who needs the evidence, what decisions it will inform, and what constraints shape how it can be used.

This utilisation-focused orientation ensures that our work generates actionable insight, not shelf reports. We match the evaluation design to the question and the context — applying experimental methods where appropriate, theory-based approaches where complexity demands it, and always grounding our work in local realities.

Pillar 2

Participatory and Inclusive

We believe that the best evidence incorporates the perspectives of those closest to the issues being studied — communities, frontline workers, local government officials, and programme participants. Our research and evaluation designs routinely include participatory methods alongside quantitative approaches.

Participation is not a checkbox. It means engaging stakeholders from question design through data collection to interpretation and use — ensuring that findings are enriched by lived experience and that the evidence is owned by those who need it most.

Pillar 3

Systems Thinking and Adaptive Management

Development challenges are complex and interconnected. We apply systems thinking to understand how programmes interact with broader institutional, social, and political dynamics. We design MEAL systems and evaluations that account for complexity — using adaptive management frameworks, feedback loops, and iterative learning processes.

Our approach draws on Collaborating, Learning, and Adapting (CLA) frameworks to help organisations navigate uncertainty and adjust strategies based on emerging evidence. We believe that the most effective programmes are those that learn as they go.

Frameworks & Standards

Methodological Foundations

Our work is anchored in internationally recognised frameworks and standards:

  • Theory of Change (ToC) — We develop and test theories of change that articulate causal pathways, assumptions, and evidence needs.
  • OECD-DAC Evaluation Criteria — All evaluations are guided by relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, and sustainability.
  • Adaptive Management & CLA — Collaborating, Learning, and Adapting frameworks are embedded in every MEAL system we design.
  • Systems Thinking — We map system dynamics, feedback loops, and interdependencies to understand programme outcomes in context.
  • Mixed Methods Research — We combine quantitative rigour with qualitative depth, matching methods to questions.
  • Participatory Approaches — Community engagement is built into our research designs, not added as an afterthought.
